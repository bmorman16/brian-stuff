
# Notes from day four Econ with John Horn

## Game Theory and Principal agent problems

### Practical application

Game theory is negotiations and how your choice impact you and the other party and the outcome depends on the decisions.  
This is the domain of the Chief Strategy Officer.  
The C suite may think this is junk science but it is really applicable.  

Never say Dominant or use that word in discussions of game theory.  It is a no regrets move and can get you into legal trouble.  
A dominated strategy is to say this will never be a good idea stop talking about it.  
Nash equilibrium - what is the expected outcome.  Where both parties end up at the best decision for them.  Stops them from making competeing choices and market finds its equilibruim.  
Backward induction - how will the competitor react?  

### What is a game?

A study of rational behavior in interactive or interdependent situations.  
More than 2 players making deliberate choices.  
More than 1 potential outcome.  
Is not a game of pure chance.  

> *KEY QUESTIONS TO ASK*
> Who are the players?  
> What do they want?  
> What can they do?  
> What do they know?  
> What do they get?  

### Simultanious games

We use a payoff matrix to describe these. (See slides for how he drew one of these up)  
Prisoners dilema is an easy example of these.  
The equilibrium of the game is for both to confess.  This gives the best outcome for both players regardless of the other person's choice.  Its a no regrets choice.
